About This Project

Libraries have enormous amounts of data in the form of text, audio, and images. These rich digital archives contain detailed, authoritative records of social, cultural, and political history of a specific period or geographical location.

Commercially available LLM chatbots can generate responses to questions on these collections; however, their answers may be unreliable or hallucinatory for several reasons. First, these models do not have direct access to the collections, nor were the materials included in their training data. Second, there is typically no systematic evaluation of the accuracy or scholarly quality of their responses.

On the contrary, Libraries provide authoritative access to these collections, positioning them uniquely to support more reliable and accountable AI-driven research tools.

In this project, we are developing a chatbot that is fine-tuned on these specific archival materials and whose responses are rigorously evaluated against a gold standard established by domain experts—historians and civil-rights scholars in our case. This approach aims to produce responses that are not only contextually relevant but also historically accurate and verifiable.

University of South Carolina Civil Rights Collections

The following collections from the University of South Carolina are included in this pilot project:

  1. New Insights in the American Civil Rights Movement: South Carolina Council on Human Relations Records The South Carolina Council on Human Relations (SCCHR) played a key role in fostering better living and social conditions for African Americans and promoting racial harmony within South Carolina and the South generally.
  2. John Henry McCray Papers, 1929–1989 This collection of manuscripts and photographs documents the life and work of journalist and politician John H. McCray (1910–1987).
  3. Modjeska Monteith Simkins Papers, 1909–1992 Columbia civil rights activist Simkins was a founder of the Victory Savings Bank and proved to be a key influence in many SC political campaigns.
  4. Briggs v. Elliott Comprised of special collections and archives from UofSC's South Caroliniana Library and South Carolina Political Collections, South Carolina Department of Archives and History, Richland County Public Library, and the S.C. State Library, this digital collection provides a contextual glance into primary sources surrounding the legal case Briggs v. Elliott.
  5. M. Hayes Mizell Papers This digital collection consists chiefly of reports relating to implementation of school desegregation that were sent to M. Hayes Mizell in his role with the American Friends Service Committee, his own speeches and writings, and photographs.
  6. James T. McCain Papers, 1957–1972 This digital collection includes calendars, notebooks, letters, publications, essays, speeches, and other papers documenting McCain's involvement with local and national civil rights organizations. The bulk of the items evidence his interest in local issues such as illiteracy, integration of public schools, and voter turnout in South Carolina's African American community.
  7. Eugene Avery Adams Papers, 1892–1968 Papers documenting the life of a twentieth-century AME minister and leader in the fields of education, civil rights, and business.
  8. Joseph A. De Laine Papers, c. 1918–2000 This core unit of three hundred fifty items covers chiefly the period from 1942, when Joseph Armstrong DeLaine submitted his annual report as secretary of the Clarendon County Citizens Committee, to 1974, when he delivered an address entitled "History leading up to the U.S. Supreme Court's Decision outlawing Segregation in Public Schools."
  9. Isaac Samuel Leevy Papers, 1905–1973 Isaac Samuel Leevy (1876–1968) was an entrepreneur and civic leader in Columbia, South Carolina best known for directing Leevy's Funeral Home and staunchly supporting a two-party political system in the state as a Republican and Richland County Chairman of the South Carolina Republican Party.
