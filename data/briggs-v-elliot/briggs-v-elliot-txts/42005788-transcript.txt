Dear George:

Your letter of recent date was received, and I was glad to hear from you.

Confirming my telephone conversation with you today, I introduced a bill concerning the appellate jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and the Circuit Courts of Appeal, and am enclosing a copy of the printed bill herein. Also find enclosed copy of the statement I made to the Senate at the time I introduced the bill.

Several months ago I requested Mr. Bob Figg to look into this matter, and the bill he prepared seems to be the most practical approach to the subject, and I am glad we are all in accord on the matter. For several days I have been conferring with Senators about it, and I am hopeful that the Judiciary Committee will report it out favorably. Please be assured of my deep interest in this matter.

It is a pleasure to cooperate with you, and please feel free to contact me at any time I can be of service to you or our State.

With best wishes,

Sincerely,
Strom Thurmond

STATEMENT BY SENATOR THURMOND (D-SC), FEBRUARY 9, 1955, UPON INTRODUCING
BILL REGARDING EXCEPTIONS TO APPELLATE JURISDICTION OF UNITED STATES
SUPREME COURT AND COURT OF APPEALS.


      The Supreme Court's decision of May 17, 1954, overturned all of the
legislative action and judicial determination on the subject during the
past ninety years, both Federal and State. Under that decision local
school authorities are required, as the price of operating public schools,
to attempt to engage in a most difficult sociological experiment.

      The Supreme Court recognized in its decision the large variety of
local conditions to which the decision must be applied. Presumably its
implementation will be left by that court to the district courts which
sit in the different localities and are familiar with the problems that
they face.

      The situation would not be helped by a flood of appeals from the
district courts in school cases. Such appeals will only clog the
appellate courts and dissipate the time and resources of the school
authorities.

      I have been studying the problem for some months, and believe that
Congress can help in a difficult situation by limiting the appellate
jurisdiction of the Federal Courts to cases involving public schools
in which inequality of a tangible nature is claimed to exist.

      The Constitution in Article III, Section 1 vests judicial power
in the Supreme Court and "in such inferior courts as the Congress
may from time to time ordain and establish."

      In Section 3 of the same article, the Constitution specifically
provides that the Congress has the power to make certain exceptions to
the appellate power of the Courts. It says:

      "In all the other cases before mentioned, the Supreme Court shall
have appellate jurisdiction, both as to law and fact, with such
exceptions, and under such regulations as the Congress shall make."

      Thus, it is clearly within the power of the Congress to limit
the appellate jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and the Court of
Appeals, as I propose in this bill.

      The enactment of this bill will strengthen the hands of both the
district courts and the school authorities in dealing with the problems
now before them. This step has been recommended by the Governor of my
State, and endorsed unanimously by the South Carolina General Assembly,
in the interest of preserving efficient public school education for all
of our people.

      I propose this bill because I believe it to be in the best interests
of all people of the United States.
