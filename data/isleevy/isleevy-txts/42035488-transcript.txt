THE COLUMBIA RECORD
Columbia, South Carolina
JOHN A. MONTGOMERY
Editor
H. HARRISON JENKINS
Associate Editor
Saturday, December 14, 1968

Isaac S. Leevy, Carolinian

A distinguished South Carolinian who served the whole people of his state with dedication, Isaac Samuel Leevy leaves his mark upon business, the school system and the community.

Instrumental in beginning many business and civic enterprises, Mrs. Leevy helped organize the Palmetto State Fair Association, found the Columbia branch of the NAACP and was a charter member of the S. C. Race Relations Commission.

The schools? He was a founder of Waverly School, the Leevy Graded School (now Carver) and Booker T. Washington. He co-organized the State Industrial School for Negro Girls and the Richland County Tuberculosis Hospital at Ridgewood.

Nor was his interest confined to the public schools. On the higher educational level, he was intensely interested in, and pushed, graduate education at State College in Orangeburg.

Before his death, he was honored by S. C. State College, Morris College, Allen University and Benedict College. Seldom has a Carolinian so earned the due accolades.

Mr. Leevy lived a long and industrious life with his days (91 years) spanning a series of staggering changes in the social structure of the state. As the milestones came and went, he could look back upon the long highway of social justice and perceive the spots where he, himself, had planted some of the markers.

As a mortician, he knew the frail mortality of man; as a man, he knew that the living must serve the living - and he did, in full measure.

MOMENT OF MEDITATION: The eye is the lamp of the body, so, if your eye is sound, your whole body will be full of light. St. Matthew 6:22. Selected by the Rev. James Rowland, vicar of The Episcopal Church of The Holy Comforter, Columbia.
