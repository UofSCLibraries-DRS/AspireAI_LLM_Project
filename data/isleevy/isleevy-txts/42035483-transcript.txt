DECEMBER 21, 1968 YOU KNOW BECAUSE YOU READ T

Leading mortician dies in Columbia

COLUMBIA, S.C. — Isaac Samuel Leevy, a funeral director here who was also a prominent educational worker, died at his home last week after a short illness.

The 91-year-old mortician was instrumental in the formation of several business and civic organizations in South Carolina. He helped start the local branch of the NAACP and was a founder of the Booker T. Washington High School, Waverly and Carver schools.

* * *

He was co-organizer of the State Industrial School, the Richland County Tuberculosis Hospital at Rodgewood and the literary department of the John C. Richards school.

He founded and served as president of the New Home Development Co., Leevy Department Stores of Columbia, Standard Furniture Co. and the Victory Savings Bank.

* * *

Mr. Leevy owned and operated the Leevy Esso Station, the first non-white in Columbia to do so. He was a key local figure of the Republican Party. He made several unsuccessful bids to state legislature and Congress. In 1964, he switched to the Democratic Party.

Leevy who was born in Antioch, Kershaw County, received his education from Antioch public schools, Mather Academy in Camden and Hampton Institute in Va.

* * *

He held honorary degrees from S.C. State College in Orangeburg, Morris College in Sumter and Allen University here. In 1967, radio station WOIC chose him Man of the Year.

Surviving him are a daughter, Mrs. Ruby Johnson of Columbia; two sons, Isaac Leevy of Columbia and Dr. Carroll Leevy of Bayonne, N.J.; four sisters, Mesdames Mamie Breece of D.C., Laural Rivers of Brooklyn, N.Y., Lucretia Bravey and Ellen Halley of Camden.
